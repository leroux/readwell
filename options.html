Reading on screens is harder than reading on paper. When you reach the end of one line, your eye has to jump back to the start of the next one, and it often lands on the wrong line. Color gradients fix this: every line ends in the color the next one starts with, so your eye follows it down the page.

Spacing, font, and line-length settings come from reading research. Extra letter and word spacing helped many readers with dyslexia in controlled studies, and shorter lines of about fifty to seventy-five characters are easier to track. Mix and match, then save what works as your own preset.
